# tutorial_galaxy_2: answers

Generated by `scripts/run_all.py` from the MaxQuant output in `results/combined/txt/` and the MSstats output of `scripts/run_msstats.R` in `msstats/`. Do not edit by hand, rerun the scripts. Definitions are documented in `scripts/mq_common.py`. Figures are written to `figures/`, which is not in git. A comparison with the tutorial's answers is at the end.

## Q1. How many proteins and features were identified in total?

**2,496 protein groups** and **228,606 features** (rows of proteinGroups.txt and evidence.txt). 84,668 features (37%) were transferred between runs by match between runs; per run between 7,761 and 13,908 features.

![How many proteins and features were identified in total?](figures/q01_proteins_features_total.png)


## Q2. In which columns (number) are the potential contaminants in the protein group and evidence file respectively?

**Column 138** in proteinGroups.txt and **column 18** in evidence.txt ("Potential contaminant", counted from 1). They flag 69 protein groups and 22,995 evidence rows. Column positions change with the MaxQuant version and the number of samples, so scripts here select columns by name.

![In which columns (number) are the potential contaminants in the protein group and evidence file respectively?](figures/q02_contaminant_columns.png)


## Q3. How large is the proportion of potential contaminants?

Potential contaminants make up **up to 62.4%** of a sample's protein group intensity (RDEB_cSCC4); the median sample has 36.8%. Almost all of it comes from contaminant groups that contain a human protein (largest: KRT14, KRT6C, KRT5); purely non-human contaminants contribute at most 2.0%. These keratins are made by the squamous tumour cells themselves, so in this tissue much of the flagged intensity is endogenous rather than contamination. Tick labels are coloured by condition.

![How large is the proportion of potential contaminants?](figures/q03_contaminant_proportion.png)


## Q4. How many proteins were removed as potential non human contaminants?

**28 protein groups** were removed: 2,496 groups before, 2,468 with a human protein after. All of them are on MaxQuant's contaminant list (CON__). Contaminant groups that contain a human protein stay, and their contaminant flag is cleared before MSstats, so MSstats does not remove them either.

![How many proteins were removed as potential non human contaminants?](figures/q04_nonhuman_contaminants_removed.png)


## Q5. How many proteins were included into the statistical analysis?

**1,698 proteins** were summarised by MSstats and entered the group comparison; 1,698 of them got a fold change. MSstats counts leading razor proteins, not protein groups: from 2,496 MaxQuant protein groups, 2,468 contain a human protein, and 1,698 leading razor proteins with at least two peptides went into MSstats.

![How many proteins were included into the statistical analysis?](figures/q05_proteins_in_statistics.png)


## Q6. Which sample has the lowest amount of proteins after protein summarization?

**RDEB_cSCC4** has the fewest proteins after summarisation: 1,092, against a median of 1,490 and a maximum of 1,571 (metast_cSCC10).

![Which sample has the lowest amount of proteins after protein summarization?](figures/q06_proteins_per_sample.png)


## Q7. In the complete experiment, how many features has a protein on average?

On average **6.2 features** were measured per protein and run (mean of NumMeasuredFeature, median 3). Over all runs together, a protein has 9.2 distinct features on average (median 5).

![In the complete experiment, how many features has a protein on average?](figures/q07_features_per_protein.png)


## Q8. Why do we filter for the adjusted p-value?

Because MSstats tests 1,643 proteins separately. With the raw p-value, about 82 proteins (5% of 1,643) would pass p < 0.05 by chance even if no protein changed; 470 pass here. The Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-value controls the false discovery rate instead: among the 181 proteins with adjusted p < 0.05, about 5% are expected to be false positives. The adjustment is more conservative, so fewer proteins pass.

![Why do we filter for the adjusted p-value?](figures/q08_adjusted_pvalue.png)


## Q9. How many proteins have a p-value below 0.05?

**181 proteins** have an adjusted p-value below 0.05. Of these, **167 are higher in metast** and **12 higher in RDEB** (log2FC beyond ±0.58, comparison metast-RDEB); 2 change less than 1.5-fold. 55 of the significant proteins were missing in one condition (infinite fold change, not shown in the volcano plot).

![How many proteins have a p-value below 0.05?](figures/q09_significant_proteins.png)


## Q10. How many proteins are differentially abundant?

**113 proteins are higher in metast** and **11 higher in RDEB** (adjusted p between 0 and 0.05, more than 1.5-fold).

Strongest in metast: APCS, ELN, CDH1, KRT19, APOE, S100A4, COL7A1, AMBP, LYZ, CLU and 103 more.

Strongest in RDEB: ASPN, COL14A1, DSC2, KRT16, ASPRV1, SPRR1B, COL12A1, COL5A2, GCC2, KRT14 and 1 more.

Proteins validated by immunostaining in the original publication:

| Gene | Publication | log2FC (metast-RDEB) | adj. p | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| COL14A1 | higher in RDEB | -2.18 | 0.03 | yes |
| APCS | higher in metast | 3.94 | 0.03 | yes |
| XRCC6 | higher in metast | 0.79 | 0.048 | yes |

![How many proteins are differentially abundant?](figures/q10_differential_proteins.png)

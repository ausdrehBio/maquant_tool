| Sample | Albumin share | Albumin rank | Other top serum, % of non-albumin | Protein groups |

The depleted sample yielded more protein identifications (248 vs 158), so depletion did gain depth.

## Q12. How much is the log2 fold change of serum albumin?

The log2 fold change of albumin (Sample1 depleted vs Sample2) is **-3.06** from Intensity, i.e. 8.4-fold lower in the depleted sample.

From LFQ intensity it is -5.19. The difference comes from normalisation: across all 144 proteins quantified in both samples, the median log2 fold change of Intensity is +3.17, so most proteins have higher intensities in the depleted sample. LFQ normalisation largely removes such a global shift; relative to the median protein, albumin changes by -6.23.

**Why Intensity instead of LFQ.** The tutorial bases Q3, Q8 and Q10 to Q12 on LFQ intensities. In MaxQuant 1.6.10 these exist even for proteins seen in only one sample, and they are barely normalised between the two samples, so they behave almost like raw intensities. MaxQuant 2.9.0 gives no LFQ value to proteins seen in one sample only, and its normalisation largely removes the global intensity shift that depletion causes. Abundance shares and the albumin fold change computed from 2.9.0 LFQ values are therefore not comparable with the tutorial; here, "Intensity" is the equivalent of the tutorial's LFQ columns. A one-off check against the tutorial's own MaxQuant output (Zenodo record 4274987) confirmed this: Intensity computed from that output gives nearly the same numbers as this run, and the tutorial's LFQ-based method reproduces its published answers there.

Generated by `scripts/run_all.py`. Do not edit by hand; rerun the scripts. Definitions (searches, layers, the reconstructed paper pipeline, matching, verdict rules) are documented in `scripts/lb_common.py`; figures are written to `figures/` and per-protein tables to `tables/`, both not in git.

Publication: Laybourn HA, Hellemann Polhaus C, Kristensen C, et al. (2024). Multi-omics analysis reveals the impact of influenza A virus host adaptation on immune signatures in pig tracheal tissue. Front Immunol 15:1432743, doi:10.3389/fimmu.2024.1432743. Data: PRIDE PXD052229 (Orbitrap Exploris 480, FAIMS, DIA; upper and lower trachea of 22 pigs 3 days after mock, swH1N1 or huH1N1 inoculation; 44 runs). The authors used Spectronaut 17.4; no protein matrix and no parameter file are deposited.

Compared: **ours** (MaxQuant 2.9.0.0 MaxDIA, library predicted from the FASTA files, results/combined/txt) in two layers, **as reported** and **run-filtered** (values only where the protein is identified in that run, as Spectronaut reports them), against **published** (Supplementary Table S3: the DEPs of six comparisons, and the paper's text). Part B compares two variants of our search: the viral FASTA files as deposited (../Laybourn_2024_depositedfasta) and a library from the pooled samples (../Laybourn_2024_poollib).

Groups: ctr = mock-inoculated (pigs 1-6); sw = swH1N1, A/swine/Denmark/2017 (pigs 7-14); hu = huH1N1, A/Denmark/238/2020 (pigs 19-26). Tissues: UT = upper, LT = lower trachea.

## Summary

**Part A: 1 of 6 checks consistent, 3 with a minor deviation, 2 inconsistent.**

- **The data and the design are the authors'.** Every swH1N1 sample is virus-positive (16/16; >= 2 viral precursors identified in the run), while 0/12 controls and 0/16 huH1N1 samples are, and all infected samples show the interferon response (Q2). Our search finds as many peptides as the paper (99.5%), fewer protein groups (85.8%; protein inference differs, Q3).
- **The biology of the interferon response reproduces**: MX1, MX2, ISG15 and IFI6 rise after both infections, viral protein is found after swH1N1 only, the upper trachea has more DEPs, and the lower trachea a larger share of antiviral DEPs (Q6).
- **The published DEP lists do not reproduce.** 72% of the published DEPs that we quantify change in the same direction, but only 11% are DEPs in our data (Q5), and 26 of 54 claim parts hold (Q6). The single-protein claims beyond the interferon response, mostly proteins that the authors saw in one group only, do not hold.
- **The published statistics explain much of this.** Missing values were set to 0 and the tests were not corrected across proteins (Q4); on our data, random group labels produce 43% as many DEPs as the real ones. Even our own searches of the same raw files agree only partly: 66% of the DEPs recur with the deposited viral FASTA (Q7) and 34% with the pool library (Q8). Lists made this way cannot be expected to agree protein by protein between analyses.

| contaminants | no contaminant database (not stated) | MaxQuant contaminants searched (True), removed before the analysis | software |

The search space is the authors': the same pig FASTA file, the same viral proteins, the same enzyme specificity and the same modifications. MaxDIA limits library peptides to 2 variable modifications, where Spectronaut's default allows 5; peptides with three or more of acetylation, oxidation and deamidation are rare, so the effect on identifications is small.

The difference that matters for the comparison is how values enter the protein table. Spectronaut, as the authors ran it, keeps a precursor's quantity in a run only where it passes the run-wise q-value filter, and the authors imputed nothing, so a protein not identified in a run has no value there. MaxDIA transfers each precursor that passes the experiment-wide FDR into every run where its q-value in that run is below 0.9, so nearly every protein has a value in nearly every run (Q2). The questions below therefore analyse two layers of our search: **as reported** and **run-filtered**, the latter keeping a value only where the protein group has a precursor with q-value <= 0.01 and PEP <= 0.1 in that run, as an approximation of the authors' processing.

Our search identifies 99.5% of the paper's peptides but only 85.8% of its protein groups. With practically the same peptides, the difference most likely lies in protein inference: MaxQuant merges proteins whose peptides are all shared into one group (the pig proteome holds many unreviewed TrEMBL isoforms of the same gene), while Spectronaut appears to keep more isoform-level groups apart (Q5 shows published accessions that sit inside larger MaxQuant groups). The two counts are therefore not a measure of sensitivity.

The paper's 3,830 'quantified' protein groups compare best with the 3,573 groups we detect in all 44 runs; the paper's 17,526 'quantified' peptides do not match our 6,680 peptides detected in all runs, so the authors' definition remains unclear. Per run, MaxDIA reports a value for 7,317 groups (median) but detects 6,345 (Q1, Q2).

Of the 444 distinct proteins in Table S3, 371 are in our protein groups. Of the published DEP entries whose protein we do not identify, 85.6% have |log2 FC| >= 4, i.e. were present in one group only in the authors' data (all entries: 57.0%).

Library: the authors' Spectronaut library from the pools holds 93,131 peptides and 10,003 proteins; our MaxDIA search of the 45 pool runs (../Laybourn_2024_pools, the pool library of Q8) identifies 64,147 peptides in 8,406 protein groups.

**Missing values were set to 0 on the log2 scale.** Two properties of Table S3 show it:

- 44.6% of the entries have |log2 FC| >= 8 and 57.0% >= 4, up to 19.26 (the viral nucleoprotein). A 2^19-fold change between two groups of tissue is not a ratio of intensities; it is the mean log2 intensity of a group against a group of zeros. With missing values left out, no fold change could be computed for a protein absent from one group.
- In 8 cases (6 proteins: GP2, HA, HEG1, NP, NPR1, SDC4) the log2 FC of 'sw vs ctr' equals that of 'sw vs hu' of the same tissue to the last digit: the protein had no value in either reference group, so both reference means are 0.

**Tukey's test.** 4 entries have an adjusted p of exactly 0 (the nucleoprotein, in UT sw vs ctr, UT sw vs hu, LT sw vs ctr, LT sw vs hu), the smallest other value is 7.6e-09. R's TukeyHSD computes p as 1 minus the studentized-range distribution function, which becomes exactly 0 once that function rounds to 1; a t-test p value would stay above 0. The adjustment covers the three pairwise comparisons of a protein, not the thousands of proteins tested, so it does not control the false discovery rate across the table (Q5 estimates what this means).

**Reconstructed pipeline** (lb_common.paper_stats): per tissue, every protein group with a value in one of its 22 samples; log2; missing values set to 0; one-way ANOVA; Tukey's HSD (k = 3 groups, 19 residual degrees of freedom); DEP at Tukey-adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 FC| >= 1. Whether the authors additionally required an ANOVA p < 0.05 cannot be seen; in our data it would remove 35 of 778 DEPs.

With the deposited files, MaxQuant reads the identifier rule >([^\s]*) (the default rule would give duplicate IDs, make_parameters_Laybourn_2024_depositedfasta.py), and the whole-segment translations of M and NS join the groups of their mature proteins. The viral evidence of HA, M1, NP and NS1 is practically the same: the cleaning changes protein IDs and grouping, not which of their peptides are found. PB1 is identified only by the deposited-FASTA search (7 peptides). All of them are also in the PB1 sequences of the our (cleaned FASTA) search, but that search reports none of them: PB1 sits at the identification threshold and comes out differently in two nearly identical MaxDIA runs. This decides the claim part 'M1, NS1, PB1, HA and NP identified' of Q6.

**Host proteins.** 94.6% of our precursor quantities per run are also in the variant, and 98.2% of these are identical (r = 0.9962). At the protein level the searches differ more: of our groups, 7,395 map one-to-one to a group of the variant, and their log2 LFQ intensities correlate at a median r = 0.966 per sample; the median difference is 0.044 log2, but 5.2% of the values differ more than 2-fold. MaxLFQ reacts to which precursors enter a group, and MaxDIA's library, decoys and machine-learning scores depend on the whole FASTA, so two searches whose FASTA files differ only in the viral entries (22 vs 26 of about 48,400) are not identical.

## Limitations

- The authors deposited neither a protein matrix nor a Spectronaut report, so the comparison stops at the published DEP lists and the text; their statistics are reconstructed from Table S3 (Q4).
- The run-filtered layer approximates Spectronaut's run-wise filtering with MaxDIA's precursor q-value and PEP per run. Spectronaut's own scoring, its protein-level run-wise q-value (0.05) and its protein quantities from filtered precursors are not reproduced; our protein quantities stay MaxQuant's LFQ.
- Spectronaut and MaxQuant group proteins differently. A published accession is matched to the MaxQuant group that contains it, which for isoform-level groups of Spectronaut is a larger group with other quantities (Q5).
- The pig numbering of the lower-trachea runs is inferred from the run numbers. The data confirm the group of every sample but not which pig of a group a lower-trachea sample comes from (Q2).
- MaxDIA results differ between nearly identical searches (Q7): single proteins near the identification or significance thresholds can come out either way.
- The GO:0051607 set is a current UniProt snapshot (annotation/), not the authors' annotation; it reproduces the published shares within 2 percentage points.
- Claims about transcripts (RNA-seq, qPCR), histology and viral load are outside this reanalysis.
